# Stage 2 equipment and research readiness

**Decision:** retain the frozen zero-shift baseline. Prioritize physics-plus-ML estimation of measured AC power; use short-horizon forecasting as the fallback. The record supports performance-change investigation, but not an identified physical ageing rate or RUL model.

Review date: 2026-10-11. No existing source, parameter, code path, or frozen output was changed. Only a diagnostic script, its tests, this report, and new diagnostic results were added. No demonstrable implementation error was found; equipment conflicts are provenance/model-validity questions.

## New rapid-transition timing test

[Offset scores](../outputs/2107_stage2_readiness/ramp_offset_scores.csv): five-minute fleet-median AC/current changes versus POA changes at t+offset, -15 to +15 minutes, on **identical exact observations**, without filling. Require all 24 AC/current channels, positive current, AC in (2,27) kW, baseline demand in [3,27) kW, QC-clear adjacent readings and positive POA at every offset. Ramp thresholds: 2 kW or 3 A. Startup/known invalid/likely clipped periods are excluded; healthy operation is not certified.

Alternate complete dates within each month/year form selection/validation sets. Selection fits a nonnegative gain and chooses offset; held-out dates use fixed gains. Temperature is not refitted; morning-afternoon gaps are not the objective.

- AC: **7,699 changes**; 3,995 selection/3,704 validation observations on 380/353 separate dates. Zero shift wins selection and validation. Held-out r=**0.938**, RMSE **1.905 kW**, rise/fall direction agreement **98.5%**. +10 has r=-0.103 and RMSE 5.474 kW; its fitted nonnegative gain is zero.
- Current independently agrees: **7,261 changes**, 3,467 held out; zero-shift r=**0.939**, RMSE **2.776 A**. Zero shift wins every represented validation year (2018-2023), every season, and both rises and falls for both signals.

**No five-minute-grid clock correction is supported.** Retain zero shift. Sub-five-minute synchronization and averaging-window start/end semantics remain unknown; spatial cloud differences can also affect ramps. The earlier POA(t+10) phase-gap improvement did not demonstrate a physical ten-minute generation delay.

AC>=29 kW accounts for **1.5-19.9%** of useful-light pairs across units/years 2018-2023; clipping guards change weather support. POA drift/recalibration, sensor plane, operational changes and shared interruptions remain confounded. Peers also hide shared decline. Permanent degradation is not established.

## Research feasibility and evaluation

| Direction | Readiness and meaningful ML role |
|---|---|
| **A. Weather-adjusted long-term decline** | **Conditional:** enough multi-year observations for robust performance-change estimates. First distinguish reversible regimes/availability from gradual change. Fit calendar/weather effects without unrestricted time terms absorbing the trend; quantify sensitivity to clipping, regimes, sensor drift and thermal proxy. Use month/year blocks and date-block uncertainty. Claim physical degradation only with calibration/maintenance evidence; simulations validate recovery of injected decline, not real ageing. |
| **B. Short-term forecasting** | **Feasible:** target future AC at 5/15/30/60 minutes; start with 5-30. Historical AC/POA/ramp features, ambient/wind and deterministic geometry/calendar exist. Optional lagged telemetry: DC current (24 units), voltage (23), AC voltage/current and 15-minute meter power. Environment has repeated hourly values despite 15-minute labels (existing audit). No future measured weather/temp_cell predictors; no archived weather forecasts, cloud imagery or rain. Day-ahead forecasting is unestablished. |
| **C. Physics-plus-ML AC estimation** | **Best supported:** use frozen physical AC plus observed contemporaneous POA/ambient/wind and solar geometry to predict measured AC. A small regularized residual model can capture nonlinear weather/phase bias; one modest tree model is a later comparison if needed. This is site-specific predictive correction, not verified optical/thermal loss identification. Do not use same-time measured DC power/current as an easy target proxy in the primary comparison. |
| **D. Lost generation / exploratory soiling** | **Conditional:** peers can support relative shortfall intervals while most units operate; physics+ML can provide uncertainty-bounded counterfactuals after independent validation. Missing POA and common fleet shutdowns weaken absolute loss estimates. No rain/cleaning logs or clean/soiled reference sensor: slow residual decline/reset is exploratory and may be shading, drift or operational change. No confirmed soiling attribution. |
| **E. RUL** | **Unsupported:** no confirmed component failures/end-of-life times, failure modes, replacement/repair history, verified healthy/degraded states, component identity/age after interventions, exposure/temperature histories, or defensible failure/censoring labels. Commissioning/start dates and QC flags supply none of these endpoints. Twenty-four correlated units at one site are not a run-to-failure fleet or 24 independent plants. |

**Primary evaluation (C):** freeze features, quality rules and targets before fitting. Use 2018-2020 training, 2021 validation, and untouched 2022-2023 test dates; exclude sparse 2017 from fitting. Compare unchanged physics, simple POA/ambient regression, then one regularized physics-residual model. Fit preprocessing only on training dates. Report MAE/RMSE/bias by inverter, season, light level, clipping and recorded-zero/nonzero slices, plus strict fleet sums/meter validation using the existing interval convention. Use date-block confidence intervals; all same-date inverters stay together. Evaluate abrupt operational changes separately; observed zeros do not automatically become normal loss training labels. Improvement cannot certify healthy counterfactual power or remove sensor uncertainty.

**Fallback evaluation (B):** start with persistence P(t+h)=P(t), smart persistence using past irradiance/clear-sky index, and simple lagged linear regression; complex ML must beat them on the same exact origins/targets. [Forecast-support counts](../outputs/2107_stage2_readiness/forecast_support.csv) give **204,516-204,518** exact fleet-power/POA-history pairs per tested horizon, **202,643-202,645** with origin weather. Require t,t-5,t-10,t-15 history and valid target power; no missing-value filling. These counts include recorded-zero operating periods. Assume origin data have arrived; logger latency/averaging support remains unverified, so operational deployment cannot yet be promised. Purge at least the 60-minute horizon across split boundaries; future measured weather is allowed only as later evaluation context, never a forecast feature.

For both directions, generalization claims are within this site. A held-out inverter is a secondary transfer check, not external-site validation. Simulated faults/decline test algorithms separately from real observations and cannot prove ageing, failure labels, or publication novelty. No model collection was trained in this review.

Two tests passed: synthetic known-delay/common-support recovery and gap-safe runs. These test mechanics, not real physical ageing.

1. **Resolve equipment/input provenance:** installed module label/revision and CEC fit report; inverter voltage/-A/input mode/settings/string schedule and cold-design/oversizing approval; POA plane, calibration and logger support. Do not silently replace the fitted row or shift POA.
2. **Run the bounded C evaluation above:** establish whether one small physics-residual model beats simple regression on unseen years and operating regimes. Keep absolute healthy/lost-power interpretation provisional.
3. **Build an operational record for A/D:** obtain maintenance, curtailment, cleaning and sensor-calibration dates; reconcile persistent/reversible jumps and common zero periods. If those records remain unavailable, use B as the fallback and defer physical ageing/soiling/RUL claims.
